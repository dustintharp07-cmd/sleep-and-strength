Sleep & Physical Strength — CDC NHANES 2011–2014

CDC NHANES 2011–2014 · N = 10,987 Individual Examinees · Ages 16 to 80+

From Athletic Recovery to Lifelong Physical Strength

Motivated by exercise-recovery research on sleep and physical performance (Craven et al., 2022) alongside clinical sleep guidelines (Watson et al., 2015), this empirical investigation examines all 10,987 U.S. adolescents, working-age adults, and older adults with complete sleep and bilateral handgrip dynamometry in CDC NHANES 2011–2014—testing how habitual weekday sleep duration aligns with objective upper-body strength across biological sex and the human lifespan.

Central Research Question & Empirical Scope

How is nightly sleep duration associated with objective physical strength, and how do biological sex and lifespan aging shape that relationship?

Methodological distinction: NHANES is a cross-sectional complex probability survey (29 strata, 61 primary sampling units, 32 design df). Throughout this site, raw participant point clouds (N = 10,987) and unweighted sample statistics are explicitly distinguished from age-adjusted and MEC survey-weighted U.S. population estimates.

Why separating sex and checking age matters: Pooling men and women places a single overall sample median (67.0 kg) in the low-density gap between male (86.8 kg sample; 89.8 kg survey-weighted) and female (55.5 kg sample; 56.3 kg survey-weighted) distributions (33.5 kg survey-weighted median gap). Within each sex, short (&le;5h, 6h) and recommended (7–8h) sleepers exhibit nearly identical median grip strength. Although 9+h sleepers show lower unadjusted sample medians (80.4 kg Male, 53.3 kg Female), clicking through the Life-Stage Filter above shows that 9+h sleepers aged 16–29 and 30–44 match 7–8h peers closely—the lower strength among 9+h sleepers concentrates in older adulthood (60–74 and 75–80+).
